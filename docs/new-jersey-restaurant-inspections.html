New Jersey restaurant inspections, explained

In New Jersey the local health department that serves each town inspects its restaurants under the state's retail food code, N.J.A.C. 8:24, and posts one of three ratings on a placard at the entrance. No statewide list exists and no department publishes its results in bulk, so Garden State Eats shows the placard only where a county posts its ratings online: 1,484 places in Monmouth and Camden counties. We compute no grade of our own.

The three placards

After an inspection, the health department rates the restaurant as N.J.A.C. 8:24-8.11 sets out, and the placard showing the rating goes where customers can see it (8:24-8.12).

  • What's posted at the entrance

    • Satisfactory the place is operating in substantial compliance with the code
    • Conditionally Satisfactory violations were found and must be corrected; a reinspection follows
    • Unsatisfactory a gross violation or an imminent health hazard; the place must stop serving until it's reinspected

One inspection is a snapshot of one day.

Who inspects each town

100 local health departments inspect the places we list: city and township departments, regional ones that contract with neighboring towns, and county departments. The state's directory of local health departments (dated October 5, 2026) names the one for each town, and Garden State Eats shows it on every place. Each department keeps its inspection reports as public records you can ask it for (N.J.A.C. 8:24-8.13).

The placards we show

Two county departments post each place's latest rating on a search page: the Monmouth County Office of Public Health and the Camden County Department of Health. We read those pages slowly, once, as their sites allow, on October 27, 2026, and matched each rating to a place by name and street address.

A place we couldn't match shows no rating, which means unknown, not uninspected. Look a place up in the Garden State Eats web app.
